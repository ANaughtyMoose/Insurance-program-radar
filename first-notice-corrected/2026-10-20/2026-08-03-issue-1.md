# FIRST NOTICE
## This Week in Programs: Landlord & Homeowners
**Issue #1 · August 3, 2026**

*Nine items from July 27 to August 3: second-quarter results from seven residential and fronting carriers, one California product launch, and Conning's new $128 billion read on the MGA channel.*

---

Slide Insurance reported a 57.6% combined ratio for the second quarter on July 28, its mostly Florida homeowners book running a 30.2% loss ratio. A day earlier, on July 27, Cincinnati Financial posted a 99.9% personal lines combined ratio and 14.4 points of current-accident-year catastrophe losses across its property-casualty book. Second-quarter earnings ran the length of the residential market this week, and catastrophe geography set most of the distance between the strongest and weakest of them.

### Launches & new products

- **Kin adds condo and full-limit private flood in California.** Kin launched an HO6 condo policy for California owners and a private flood endorsement for its existing home policies, the flood cover carrying no 30-day waiting period and matching the home policy's full dwelling and personal-property limits instead of the National Flood Insurance Program's $250,000 building cap. The release put California's expected annual flood losses at $11.73 billion and counted 684,000 residents now on the FAIR Plan; Kin writes in 14 states. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kin-expands-california-homeowner-focus-with-new-condo-and-flood-insurance-options-302836174.html))
  → **For MGA operators and wholesalers:** Kin's full-limit private flood with no waiting period is a concrete spec to beat for anyone placing residential flood against the NFIP's $250,000 cap, and it comes bundled to a book Kin already underwrites in the state.

### Capacity & fronting

- **Conning puts the US MGA channel at $128 billion.** Conning estimated US managing general agent premium reached about $128 billion in 2025, above the $102.6 billion of MGA direct premium written that carriers reported to the NAIC (itself up 12% from 2024), with the gap reflecting Lloyd's and other premium outside statutory reporting. Fronting carriers generated roughly $22.6 billion in gross premium in 2025, and Conning estimates about 20% of US MGA premium runs through fronting relationships. ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/conning-estimates-us-mga-premiums-reached-128bn-in-2025/))
  → **For fronting carriers and PE-VC investors:** Conning's $22.6 billion fronting figure sizes the fee pool Hippo, Accelerant, and their competitors are pricing against.

- **SiriusPoint's book runs an 88.5% combined ratio with almost no catastrophe loss.** SiriusPoint reported second-quarter net income of $68.6 million available to common shareholders on July 29, an 88.5% consolidated combined ratio (91.4% core), just $1.3 million of catastrophe losses, and a 12.0% return on equity; its Insurance & Services gross premium, the segment that backs MGAs, grew 15%. ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/29/3335588/0/en/siriuspoint-reports-second-quarter-2026-net-income-of-69m-return-on-equity-of-12-0-and-operating-return-on-equity-of-13-8.html))
  → **For MGA operators:** a program capacity provider posting a sub-90 combined ratio with $1.3 million of quarterly catastrophe losses has room and reason to add programs.

### Market entries & movers

- **Slide prints a 57.6% combined ratio on its mostly Florida homeowners book.** Slide Insurance reported second-quarter net income of $134.9 million, up 92.4% year over year, on a 57.6% combined ratio that improved 980 basis points, a 30.2% loss ratio (from 37.4%), and gross premiums written of $508.0 million, up 16.7%. ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/28/3334736/0/en/Slide-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.html))
  → **For state entrants and PE-VC investors:** Slide's 30.2% loss ratio is what a clean post-reform Florida homeowners book earns in a light catastrophe quarter, the benchmark any investor pricing a Florida entry or a takeout vehicle now has to beat.

- **Cincinnati Financial takes 14.4 points of catastrophe losses.** Cincinnati Financial reported a 100.8% property-casualty combined ratio for the second quarter on July 27, with personal lines at 99.9% and 14.4 points of current-accident-year catastrophe losses across the book; net written premiums grew 3%. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cincinnati-financial-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-302835558.html))
  → **For reinsurers and program cedents:** Cincinnati's personal lines carried 21.4 catastrophe points (from 25.4) and still improved to a 99.9% combined ratio, against Slide's 57.6%.

- **The Hanover's personal lines combined ratio improves to 88.9%.** The Hanover Insurance Group reported record second-quarter net income of $191.6 million on July 28, a personal lines combined ratio of 88.9% (from 95.5% a year earlier), catastrophe losses of $91.8 million or 5.7 points (down from 7.0), and net premiums written up 4.6% to $1.66 billion, with personal lines premium up 2.6% to $697.6 million. ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/hanover-posts-record-q2-income-with-91-2-combined-ratio-584148.aspx))
  → **For HNW incumbents and program administrators:** Hanover cut its personal lines combined ratio 6.6 points while growing that premium only 2.6%, a rate-and-selection gain that shows the admitted personal lines book repricing faster than it is expanding.

- **Hippo grows quarterly profit to $10 million and raises full-year guidance.** Hippo Holdings reported second-quarter net income of $10 million on July 30, gross written premium of $482.2 million (up 61.5%), a 95.8% combined ratio (four points better), and revenue of $144.7 million (up 23.4%); it raised full-year 2026 guidance to $1.65 to $1.7 billion of gross written premium, $62 to $70 million of adjusted net income, and a 99% to 101% combined ratio, from 103% to 105%. ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hippo-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-302838160.html))
  → **For fronting carriers and MGA operators:** Hippo, set to become Accelerant's US fronting carrier in 2027, is now showing a sub-100 combined ratio on its own underwriting, so the balance sheet MGAs will rent from it is turning profitable.

- **Porch's reciprocal exchange grows policies 38%.** Porch Group reported second-quarter reciprocal written premium of $139.8 million at the Porch Reciprocal Exchange, with reciprocal policies written up 38% year over year to 58,700, Insurance Services adjusted EBITDA of $44.4 million (from $19.7 million a year earlier), and gross profit reaching 58% of reciprocal written premium, up from 48%; the reciprocal itself posted a $19.8 million net loss while Porch shareholders recorded $5.6 million of net income. ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/porch-groups-insurance-margin-doubles-as-reciprocal-policies-grow-38-584371.aspx), [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1784535/000178453526000046/prch-20260729xexx991.htm))
  → **For PE-VC investors and state entrants:** the Porch Reciprocal Exchange shows the reciprocal-exchange model with policies written up 38% while the management company keeps the fee, the same structure new Florida carriers keep adopting.

- **Lemonade's homeowners loss ratio falls to 44%.** Lemonade reported a 44% gross loss ratio on its homeowners multi-peril book for the second quarter on July 29, down from 60% a year earlier, with homeowners in-force premium of $554 million; company-wide in-force premium reached $1.43 billion (up 32%) and the overall gross loss ratio was 60% (from 67%), with catastrophes adding 3 points. Lemonade cut its quota-share cession from about 20% to 18% effective July 1. ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000047/lmndshareholderletterq22.htm))
  → **For reinsurers and ILS investors:** Lemonade kept 2 more points of its whole book (cession cut to about 18% from 20%) in the quarter its homeowners loss ratio fell to 44% from 60%.

### The Take

Second-quarter results showed two things at once. The reform-era Florida book posted low loss ratios, with Slide at a 57.6% combined ratio and 30.2% loss ratio, while diversified national homeowners paper absorbed spring convective storms, with Cincinnati at 21.4 personal lines catastrophe points and a 99.9% personal lines ratio. Underneath both, Hippo grew quarterly profit and raised guidance, policies written at the Porch Reciprocal Exchange were up 38%, and Conning sized the MGA channel at $128 billion. If you front programs, Hippo's move to a sub-100 combined ratio sets the profitability bar your MGA partners will hold you to, so re-underwrite any program whose loss ratio is drifting toward Cincinnati's cat-loaded book. If you run an MGA, SiriusPoint's 88.5% combined ratio and Conning's 12% channel growth say program capacity is profitable, so lock multi-year terms now and price convective-storm exposure into any geographically diversified book before the January renewal. If you allocate capital, Slide's 30.2% loss ratio and the Porch Reciprocal Exchange, with policies written up 38%, are the cleanest public comps for the takeout carrier and the reciprocal exchange, the two structures Florida entrants keep choosing; underwrite the catastrophe geography before the multiple. Mercury General is due to report August 4 and Kingstone August 6, both homeowners-heavy, and their catastrophe points will show whether Cincinnati's 21.4 personal lines catastrophe points were a national convective quarter.
