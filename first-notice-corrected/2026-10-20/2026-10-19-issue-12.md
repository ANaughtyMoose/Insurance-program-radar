### Capacity & fronting

- **Post-landfall estimates put Hurricane Isaias between $1 billion and the mid-single-digit billions, at or near the low end of what the brokers expected.** Cotality opened the post-landfall estimates on October 12 at $2 billion to $3 billion of industry insured wind and storm surge loss. Verisk followed on October 13 at $1 billion to $2 billion for onshore property, with wind expected to account for the vast majority, covering residential, commercial and industrial property plus automobiles and privately insured storm surge, and excluding precipitation-induced flooding, National Flood Insurance Program losses, loss adjustment expenses and tornadoes spawned by the storm. Swiss insurance-linked securities manager Euler ILS Partners put the industry loss at $2 billion to $4 billion the same day, said "Current analysis indicates no impact on the portfolio" while noting the losses "may add to aggregate structures," and said loss assessment is "particularly challenging because most damage resulted from complex, highly localised atmospheric turbulence." Fitch estimated on October 13 that "industry losses could reach the mid-single-digit billions of dollars." Karen Clark & Company estimated about $3 billion of privately insured loss on October 15, which Insurance Journal reported as comparable to Hurricane Idalia, a Category 3 storm that hit a less-populated part of the Florida Panhandle in 2023, against an estimated $56 billion for Hurricane Ian in 2022. Moody's RMS closed the week on October 16 at $2.5 billion to $4.5 billion with a best estimate of $3.5 billion, of which storm surge, inland flooding and National Flood Insurance Program losses account for about $250 million, using Version 25 of its North Atlantic Hurricane Models and Version 1.3 of its US Inland Flood HD Model. Jeff Waters, Director of North Atlantic Hurricane Models at Moody's, said "we expect the overall extent and severity of insured losses from this event to be manageable for the market," and Raj Vojjala, Managing Director of Modeling and Analytics, attributed losses to "a high incidence of treefall-driven roof damage in the affected area" alongside possible roof replacements under Florida's 25% roof rule. The storm made landfall near Destin, Florida at approximately 8:30 p.m. Central on October 9 as a Category 2 with 105 mph sustained winds and a minimum central pressure of 971 millibars, the ninth named storm of the 2026 Atlantic season. Before landfall, Gallagher Re had put losses in the mid-single-digit billions including National Flood Insurance Program gross claims, BMS Group's Andrew Siffert had raised his range to $4 billion to $6 billion on October 9 from $3 billion to $5 billion a day earlier, and Marsh Re had said mid-single digits of billions. ([Insurance Business on Cotality](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/catastrophe/insurers-mobilize-as-hurricane-isaias-loss-estimate-comes-in-at-23-billion-593232.aspx), [Verisk](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/13/3379998/0/en/verisk-estimates-insured-losses-for-hurricane-isaias-could-reach-usd-2-billion.html), [Artemis on Euler ILS](https://www.artemis.bm/news/hurricane-isaias-insurance-industry-loss-estimated-in-2bn-4bn-range-euler-ils-partners/), [Reinsurance News on Fitch](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/mid-single-digit-billions-of-dollars-loss-from-isaias-unlikely-to-affect-pc-re-insurer-credit-profiles-says-fitch/), [Insurance Journal on KCC](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/10/15/889051.htm), [Artemis on Moody's RMS](https://www.artemis.bm/news/moodys-estimates-insured-losses-from-hurricane-isaias-in-2-5bn-to-4-5bn-range/), [Reinsurance News on Moody's RMS](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/moodys-rms-says-hurricane-isaias-insured-losses-could-hit-4-5bn/), [pre-landfall broker estimates](https://www.artemis.bm/news/hurricane-isaias-forecast-to-hit-cat-3-then-weaken-single-digit-billion-insured-losses-still-likely/))
  → **For reinsurers/ILS and fronting carriers:** Every post-landfall estimate came in at or below BMS's final $4 billion to $6 billion pre-landfall range, with only Moody's RMS's $4.5 billion top end inside it, and Moody's RMS attributing the loss to treefall roof damage and Florida's 25% roof rule puts the severity in roof replacement, which is where a Panhandle homeowners program's deductible structure either holds or fails.

- **Panhandle claims ran in the hundreds and low thousands, and AM Best calls Isaias an earnings event.** Three Florida carriers gave Panhandle claim counts as of Monday, October 12. People's Trust Insurance reported just over 400 claims out of about 8,000 insureds, Universal Property & Casualty about 3,000 claims on about 20,000 policies, and Security First about 300 claims out of about 15,000 policies in force, of which about five were considered severe. Melissa Burt DeVriese, president of Security First Insurance, compared the storm to Hurricane Michael in 2018, which cost insurers about $9 billion in Florida claims or about $12 billion in today's dollars, and said "So, about a tenth of that would make Isaias a billion-dollar storm." Matt Mercier, national director of community associations for CBIZ, said "So much of what we have seen has been below the deductible." Florida Citizens held about 8,800 policies in the ten-county Panhandle area at landfall per Office of Insurance Regulation data and had received 860 claims by October 15, mostly residential. AM Best expects Isaias to be an "earnings event" rather than a capital or solvency event, said it is "not expected to significantly deplete surplus levels" in a way that alters industry-wide Florida capacity, and said the depopulation of Citizens and a mild season so far will keep Citizens intact as the state's insurer of last resort. Fitch said on October 13 that it "does not expect Isaias' losses to exhaust catastrophe reinsurance protection for most Florida specialists; however, individual insurers with unique risk concentrations may report higher-than-expected gross losses," flagged newer unrated Florida specialists that built capital in improved conditions as "relatively untested by a major catastrophe event" and vulnerable where losses exceed their reinsurance limits, and said national primary insurers will keep a larger share of wind losses in Alabama and other affected states than in Florida because they use less reinsurance there. The strongest winds of 105 to 117 mph struck the western eyewall around Pensacola, Panama City Beach recorded 5.6 feet of surge per Gallagher Re, and 21 tornadoes were confirmed across the Southeast. The Office of Insurance Regulation had published no statewide claim figures as of October 19. ([Insurance Journal, October 13](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/10/13/888686.htm), [Insurance Journal, October 15](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/10/15/889051.htm), [Reinsurance News on Fitch](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/mid-single-digit-billions-of-dollars-loss-from-isaias-unlikely-to-affect-pc-re-insurer-credit-profiles-says-fitch/))
  → **For fronting carriers and reinsurers/ILS:** Fitch put the exposure on unrated Florida specialists with losses above their limits and on national primaries retaining Alabama wind.

### Rate & regulatory

- **October 15 at the California FAIR Plan: dwelling fire rates up 29.1%, renewal commission down to 3%.** The California FAIR Plan's approved average dwelling fire rate increase of 29.1% took effect on October 15, applied at renewal, under California Department of Insurance file 25-2174 and SERFF filing MOCC-134657692, which closed on April 6, 2026. The Plan's commission restructuring, announced on August 17, applies to policies with an effective date on or after October 15 and cuts new business commission from 10% to 7% and renewal commission from 8% to 3%. The affected lines are Dwelling Fire, Commercial, Commercial High Value and Businessowners, and California Earthquake Authority business is unaffected. The FAIR Plan said it determined that restructuring broker commissions was necessary to support its long-term financial stability, citing growth in policies in force as traditional insurers scale back, and said recently approved premium increases are expected to offset the financial impact. The Plan had 696,562 policies in force as of June 2026, a 157% increase since September 2022, with about $768 billion of exposure and about $2 billion of written premium ([filing detail](https://nathanbwittasek.substack.com/p/fair-plan-rates-rise-today)). Hardening discounts run up to 16.4% off the wildfire portion of premium, and because the increase is weighted toward that portion, some policyholders will see decreases. No primary FAIR Plan bulletin was available, so these figures rest on trade and agent-association reporting, and no source confirms the commission change was implemented as scheduled or deferred. ([First Connect](https://www.firstconnectinsurance.com/blog/california-fair-plan-rate-increase-commission-cuts-agents/), [American Agents Alliance](https://agentsalliance.com/california-fair-plan-announces-major-broker-commission-cuts/))
  → **For wholesalers and state entrants:** A producer renewing FAIR Plan dwelling fire would earn 3% on a premium that just rose 29.1%, a different calculation for keeping any of the Plan's 696,562 policies in the residual market.

- **Colorado began enforcing wildfire risk-score disclosure and a formal appeal clock on homeowners underwriting.** The Colorado Division of Insurance announced on October 12 that homeowners protections and transparency rights under House Bill 25-1182, the state's risk model law, have taken effect. Insurers using wildfire risk scores for rating or underwriting must consider qualified property-level and community-level mitigation, and insurers that do not build mitigation into their models must give discounts to policyholders who document qualified work. Insurers must send a written notice carrying the property's wildfire risk score or classification, a plain-language explanation of how it was calculated, the full range of possible scores, the features that affected it, the effect of mitigation and notice of the right to appeal. The notice is due within 15 days of a completed application for new applicants and with the renewal offer or the nonrenewal notice for existing policyholders, and those disclosures began on October 1. On appeal, the insurer or the FAIR Plan must review mitigation evidence and update the score within 30 days, acknowledge a formal appeal within 10 calendar days and issue a final written reconsideration decision within 30 calendar days, continuing coverage at least 10 calendar days past that decision if it runs beyond policy expiration. The Division states that the requirements established by House Bill 25-1182 apply to admitted insurers and the Colorado FAIR Plan and do not apply to non-admitted or surplus lines insurers. New Regulation 5-1-29, covering homeowners filing requirements for wildfire models and mitigation discounts, takes effect January 1, 2027. Commissioner Michael Conway said the law requires insurers to be "open with homeowners about how wildfire mitigation can lead to potential savings." The Division cited CSAA General Insurance Company offering a 15% discount on the wildfire portion of premium for homes with an IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home Enhanced or Essential designation. ([Colorado Division of Insurance](https://doi.colorado.gov/news-releases-consumer-advisories/new-homeowners-insurance-protections-and-transparency-rights), [Regulation 5-1-29 adoption notice](https://doi.colorado.gov/announcements/notice-of-adoption-emergency-regulation-26-e-04-and-new-regulation-5-1-29))
  → **For MGA operators and program administrators:** On admitted paper a Colorado wildfire risk comes with the 15-day disclosure and the 10-day and 30-day appeal clocks; written non-admitted, House Bill 25-1182 does not apply, and Regulation 5-1-29 filing requirements arrive January 1, 2027.

- **Florida ordered insurers to report Isaias claims twice in three days, surplus lines included.** The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation issued a data call notice for Hurricane Isaias catastrophe reporting on October 12 under section 624.307, Florida Statutes, with submission deadlines of Tuesday, October 13 and Thursday, October 15, data due by 12 p.m. Eastern for each. The call binds all insurers authorized to write any of 20 named Florida lines, including Homeowners Multi-Peril, Fire, Allied Lines, Farmowners Multi-Peril, Commercial Multi-Peril, Private Flood, Mobile Home Multi-Peril, Mobile Home Physical Damage, Surplus Lines Property and Casualty, and Surplus Lines Federally Authorized. Insurers file on the simplified 2026 Catastrophe Reporting Form through the Insurance Regulation Filing System, on an individual insurer basis, cumulatively as of each reporting date, and insurers with no Florida policies in force in the listed lines, or policies but no claims, must file a one-time "No Data" report. Each filer must download a fresh template because previously downloaded templates will not work. The notice carries no reference number and no quotes, and the Office had published no aggregated results as of October 19. ([Florida OIR data call notice](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/949086156/data-call-notice-hurricane-isaias-catastrophe-reporting), [OIR newsroom](https://floir.gov/newsroom/archives))
  → **For program administrators and wholesalers:** A surplus lines property program writing Florida owed the same cumulative claim file as an admitted homeowners carrier, by noon on October 13 and again on October 15, on a template pulled fresh for each submission.

### The litigation layer

- **Louisiana federal courts are staying bad-faith suits for defective pre-suit notice, and the state Supreme Court has the question.** Nicholas P. Arnold, a partner at Blue Williams LLC, documented a line of Eastern District of Louisiana decisions enforcing La. R.S. 22:1892.2 in an analysis published October 14. The statute, created by Act 3 of 2024, requires an insured to give 60 days' written notice of the alleged violation before suing over certain catastrophic losses involving immovable property, and provides for an automatic stay where suit is filed before proper notice. In Castro-Rochez v. Allied Trust Insurance Co., docket 2:2025cv02431, the court held that the insureds' demand package, which included an engineering report and an estimate, described the damage but failed to notify the insurer of an alleged statutory violation, and stayed the suit. In 3612 Edenborn LLC v. Chaucer Insurance Co. DAC the insured argued that a repair estimate sent more than 60 days before suit satisfied the requirement, and the court disagreed and stayed the case until adequate notice was given. In Sicilian Foods LLC v. Atain Specialty Insurance Co. another Eastern District judge enforced the stay and explained how the cure period lets an insurer reduce statutory penalty exposure. Arnold wrote that "The emerging cases show that sending an estimate and giving notice of bad faith may be two different acts," and said the Louisiana Supreme Court is considering a writ application on the issue. The analysis gives no decision dates, no judge names and no dollar amounts, and two of the three captions appear commercial. ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/10/14/340641.htm))
  → **For MGA operators and fronting carriers:** In the Eastern District of Louisiana a repair estimate has not counted as notice of a statutory violation, which gives the insurer its 60-day cure window, and a writ application before the Louisiana Supreme Court could settle the point.

### The Take

Private buyers paid up for residential program platforms and the capital behind them in the same week the most recent homeowners MGA IPO traded below its own offer price. Artemis reported on October 12 that Pillar Capital Management manages $4.3 billion and that TransRe has exited, after SageSure's stake was announced. Porch Group and the Porch Insurance Reciprocal Exchange agreed on October 14 to pay $95 million for Allied Trust's managing general agency and captive reinsurer and $55 million for its carrier, plus $50 million deferred, for about $200 million of reciprocal written premium and a Louisiana book. Saga Ventures priced Stand at $1 billion on October 15 on $10 billion of insured property value, and Baldwin's October 15 proxy put $7.7 billion behind a $32.50 per share take-private. Against all of that, Orion180 traded at $8.95, its close before the October 13 initiations, while five firms initiated coverage at $12.90 to $20.00, every target above the $12.00 it priced at a month earlier. The capacity underneath these businesses kept getting cheaper. SafePoint guided the Class A tranche of Nature Coast Re 2026-2 at 6.75% to 7.50% on October 16 against the 8% spread it paid in February 2026 on a slightly higher expected loss. Hurricane Isaias did nothing to interrupt that, with Verisk at $1 billion to $2 billion, Karen Clark & Company at about $3 billion, Moody's RMS at a $3.5 billion best estimate and AM Best calling it an earnings event. Regulators spent the week on the price and the paperwork of homeowners underwriting. The California FAIR Plan's 29.1% dwelling fire increase and its cut in renewal commission from 8% to 3% share an October 15 effective date, Colorado's House Bill 25-1182 disclosure and appeal clocks are now running ahead of Regulation 5-1-29 on January 1, 2027, and Florida collected Isaias claim files from admitted and surplus lines writers alike by noon on October 13 and October 15.

If you front programs, set your January 1 ceded-cost assumption against SafePoint's Class AA guidance of 4.00% to 4.75% on a 0.73% expected loss, and take Fitch's October 13 point seriously that national primaries will retain a larger share of Alabama wind losses than of Florida losses because they buy less reinsurance there. If you run an MGA, build the Colorado disclosure and appeal workflow now, because the 15-day notice and the 10-day and 30-day appeal clocks under House Bill 25-1182 bind admitted paper today while Regulation 5-1-29's filing requirements arrive January 1, 2027. If you allocate capital, note that Allied Trust's agency and captive changed hands at a $95 million base for about $25 million of expected Adjusted EBITDA, about 3.8 times, a figure to set beside the multiple implied by Orion180's public market value.
